Could a private equity sponsor take Domino’s Pizza Group private — and generate a 20% return?

I built an independent, five-year leveraged buyout (LBO) model of **Domino’s Pizza Group plc (LSE: DOM)**, using its UK annual report, interim results and publicly available market data.

One of the most useful findings was that **the deal did not work at my initial acquisition assumptions**.

For a hypothetical **30% takeover premium** over the **231p** share price on 6 November 2026, the analysis implies:

• **£1,446m** acquisition enterprise value, equivalent to **10.6x** LTM underlying EBITDA
• **3.0x** opening debt / LTM EBITDA and **£1,084m** sponsor equity investment
• **1.44x** gross MOIC and **7.5%** five-year gross sponsor IRR in the base case
• A maximum implied **£707m** acquisition equity price to target a 20% gross IRR — **below the undisturbed market capitalisation** of approximately £889m

Rather than adjusting assumptions to produce an attractive return, I stress-tested the operating forecast, entry/exit multiples and leverage, and explored why the downside case matters so much to the investment committee.

The project includes a formula-linked Excel LBO model, debt sweep, three operating scenarios, return sensitivities and an investment committee memo. It also prompted me to think carefully about lease accounting, existing debt, refinancing risks and the difference between the UK-listed master franchisee and the US parent.

**My conclusion: do not proceed at the illustrative 30% premium under the base-case underwriting.**
